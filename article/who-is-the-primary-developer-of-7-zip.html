Who Is the Primary Developer of 7-Zip?

This article identifies the creator behind 7-Zip and explores his contributions to the world of data compression. It covers who developed the software, the core technologies he engineered alongside it, and how he continues to maintain the open-source utility today.

The primary developer responsible for creating 7-Zip is the Russian software engineer Igor Pavlov. He first released the free, open-source file archiver in 1999, and he has remained the project's lead developer and maintainer ever since.

In addition to building the 7-Zip software itself, Pavlov created the 7z archive format and developed the LZMA (Lempel-Ziv-Markov chain algorithm) and LZMA2 compression methods. These algorithms are known for achieving exceptionally high compression ratios, outperforming older standard formats like ZIP and GZIP while maintaining fast decompression speeds. Pavlov placed the LZMA SDK into the public domain, allowing other software developers worldwide to integrate his compression technology into their own applications.

Pavlov maintains 7-Zip largely as an independent project. The software is distributed under the GNU Lesser General Public License (LGPL), making it free for both personal and commercial use without licensing fees.

Despite the global popularity of 7-Zip, Pavlov maintains a low public profile. He does not run a high-profile corporate entity, nor does he actively engage in self-promotion. Instead, he primarily communicates through the official 7-Zip discussion forums hosted on SourceForge, where he directly answers technical questions, addresses bug reports, and publishes updates for new releases.
